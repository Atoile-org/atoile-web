# About Us

## Your device. Your rules. Your software.

We're a collective defending a simple principle: when you buy a device, it's yours. Really yours. Not half-yours, not conditionally yours, not yours only as long as you stay locked inside the manufacturer's ecosystem.

That basic idea has somehow become radical. Locked bootloaders, spare parts crippled by "parts pairing," updates that strip away features after a year of use, prices that shift depending on your phone or your location, sensors quietly reporting data you never agreed to share. Dispossession has been normalized, that's why we've decided to push back.

We work on three fronts: we **advocate** to change laws and industry practice, we **inform** people about what's actually happening, and we **build** free tools so everyone has a way out, starting now, without waiting for the law to catch up.

---

## The right to own your devices

A device you can't repair, modify, or understand isn't really yours, it's a lease disguised as a purchase.

We advocate for:

- **Unlocked bootloaders** on every device, with no unreasonable trade-off or loss of warranty
- **Control over your operating system**, the ability to install the OS of your choice, and pressure on manufacturers to open-source or document their drivers so that choice is actually possible in practice
- **The right to repair**: spare parts available at fair prices, accessible diagrams and diagnostic tools, and an end to software locks that make a legitimate replacement part "invalid" in the eyes of the device
- **An end to planned obsolescence**, hardware and software alike, devices should get a guaranteed minimum period of security support, not get abandoned the moment the next model ships
- **The right to resell or give away** a device without it being crippled or degraded once it changes hands
- **Interoperability**, the freedom to switch providers or repair shops without losing access to your own features or data

---

## The right to privacy

Surveillance isn't the exception anymore, it's become the default business model. We believe it's your call what you share, not the platform's.

We advocate for:

- **Protection against tracking**, whether it's ad-based tracking or fingerprinting, consent-driven or extracted through deceptive interfaces
- **An end to personalized pricing**: when the price you're shown depends on your device, your location, or your browsing history, that's quiet discrimination most people never even realize is happening to them
- **Protection against mass surveillance**, public or privatized, facial recognition and sensor networks deployed without real democratic debate are a genuine risk, even when they're sold to us as "just for safety"
- **End-to-end encryption by default**, with no backdoors, "for the authorities" or otherwise
- **Real data portability**, the ability to export your data and leave without artificial friction

---

## Our tools

We don't just ask nicely. While the law catches up with technical reality, we build free, open tools so people can take back control right now.

**Our first project** is a browser extension currently in development, aimed at neutralizing a widespread but rarely discussed practice: dynamic pricing based on your device's data. By altering the signals sent to websites, location, operating system, hardware configuration, the goal is simple: make you invisible to the algorithms that quietly push prices up based on who you are or where you're connecting from.

This project is only the beginning. Others will follow, all built on the same principle: free and open-source software, auditable code, and zero data collection on our end.

## Campaigns & watchdogging

We publicly document what large companies would rather keep quiet.

- **GDPR non-compliance**: we track companies that drag their feet or flatly refuse to cooperate with data protection authorities, along with the sanctions actually enforced by the CNIL and its European counterparts
- **Privatized surveillance**: cases like Flock Safety in the United States show where mass surveillance ends up when it's deployed without a real democratic framework. Even though it doesn't directly concern us today, we document these developments so they never become the norm in France
- **Attacks on device ownership**: scandals like *Google & Keep Android Open* show how an ecosystem can quietly close in on itself, almost without anyone noticing, until real choice disappears for the end user

Our goal isn't to react after the fact, it's to make visible what's being put in place *before* it becomes a structural problem.

---

## Why now

None of this is set in stone. Unlocked bootloaders have existed before. The right to repair is already advancing in some countries. GDPR, flaws and all, shifted the balance of power. But none of it is guaranteed either, it has to be defended, actively, continuously.

That's what we're here to do.
